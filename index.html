How to read these numbers

        
    
          
  • A 24GB device is the natural unit for one Q4 worker of a ~27B model at modest context; the selected model changes that threshold.
    • 
          
  • Two GPUs can improve precision or host two independent workers; those are different experiments.
    • 
          
  • RTX 5090 cards have 32GB each and no NVLink. Independent workers avoid PCIe sharding overhead; a BF16 model of this class requires at least two cards.
    • 
          
  • RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell cards provide 96GB ECC each. Workstation Edition favors one fast 600W card; 300W Max-Q is the practical dense two- or four-GPU path.
    • 
          
  • For harness research, independent workers are usually more valuable than splitting one small model across many GPUs.
    • 
          
  • The M4 Air remains useful as the router, interface, telemetry collector, and small-model worker.
    • 
          
  • The Phase split selector covers every current Apple-sold Mac chip, GPU, and unified-memory combination except MacBook Neo, deliberately excluded because its fixed 8GB cannot launch a 27B-class model.
    • 
          
  • Buy and every harness topology read exact Mac configurations from one shared catalog, so memory and bandwidth assumptions stay consistent across shapes.
    • 
          
  • Storage, finishes, port bundles, and screen sizes are collapsed when they do not change the machine’s model-fit or inference-bandwidth profile.
    • 
          
  • Air, base M5, and M5 Pro MacBooks are usually control-plane or whole-request-routing machines here; M5 Max is the first MacBook tier with a strong enough bandwidth contrast to merit a measured Spark-prefill / Mac-decode trial.
    • 
          
  • Mini, Studio, and Spark clusters should default to complete model replicas; distributed single-model execution is a separate, communication-sensitive experiment.
    • 
          
  • Cross-platform prefill/decode is a third mode: phase-matched and promising, but not equivalent to pooling Mac and Spark memory.
    • 
        

      

      

        
Sources and uncertainty

        
Hardware facts come from Qwen’s official Qwen3.8 repository, the official 27B model card, Apple’s current MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, iMac, Mac mini, and Mac Studio matrices. Configuration compatibility is cross-checked against Apple’s detailed M4 Air, M5 Air, M5 MacBook Pro, M5 Pro / Max MacBook Pro, two-port iMac, four-port iMac, Mac mini, and Mac Studio specifications; Apple’s M3 Ultra announcement documents the 512GB ceiling. NVIDIA facts come from the official RTX 5090 specifications, RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell family specifications, and the DGX Spark hardware guide.

        
The EPYC builder derives its SP3 lane and memory assumptions from AMD’s EPYC 7003 architecture and the ASRock Rack ROMED8-2T; its SP5 workstation is based on the Supermicro H13SSL-NT. Exact SP5 CPU values come from AMD’s official 9254, 9354P, and 9454P specifications. The validated eight-GPU topology follows the ASUS ESC8000A-E13. Power, PSU headroom, circuit class, cooling, and consumer-GPU compatibility remain planning estimates.

        
Cluster behavior is grounded in the official MLX distributed communication guide and NVIDIA’s DGX Spark clustering guide. Phase-split guidance distinguishes EXO’s measured Mac + Spark demonstration from the public EXO runtime and its still-open Metal + CUDA work.

        
Quantized weight sizes, context reserves, concurrency, fit grades, and relative investment bands are planning estimates as of August 20, 2026. Dollar-sign bands are comparative and exclude facility, cooling, and used-market volatility. Validate the exact backend and quant before purchasing hardware.
